About

Most tools ask "is this a known bad zone?" GHAST asks "does this vessel make physical sense?"

A lot of maritime spoofing and jamming tools work the way an antivirus scanner works: they keep a list — known jamming zones, known bad actors, known patterns — and raise an alert when something on the list shows up. That works right up until something doesn't match the list. A new jamming source outside a mapped zone, a spoofing technique nobody's cataloged yet, a vessel behaving strangely for a reason no one's seen before — none of that trips a signature check.

GHAST starts from a different question. Given everything a vessel has just told the world about itself — its speed, its course, how fast it can physically turn — where should it be next? When the position it actually reports drifts too far from that answer, the list it's on stops mattering. The deviation is the evidence, whether or not anyone has seen this particular flavor of spoofing before.

What we do differently

01
Behavior, not a blocklist
A physics-bounded next-state prediction catches spoofing that doesn't match any known signature or mapped jamming zone — because it never needed to match one.
02
It tells you which kind of problem you have
A single flagged vessel and a whole area flagging at once look the same as a raw alert. GHAST's fleet-wide check separates targeted spoofing from area jamming automatically, before an analyst opens the incident.
03
An evidence trail, not a black-box score
Every tool the investigation agent calls and everything it finds is logged. The output is a hypothesis with attached evidence and a confidence number — never a bare "this is spoofing."
04
Confidence gates escalation, not alarm volume
Low-confidence or novel patterns go to a human analyst with partial evidence instead of getting auto-declared. False-positive rate — the metric that actually erodes an insurer's trust — is treated as a first-class number from Stage 1 onward, not an afterthought.

Where this is now

An academic project, built like a real one

GHAST is currently Stage 1 of a three-semester build at GTU-SET: a live AIS ingestion pipeline, a Bi-LSTM trajectory model benchmarked against published baselines and tracked in MLflow, and a rule-assisted investigation agent with three working tools. The site around it is honest about that — the diagrams and reports you've seen are illustrative, not a live feed, and they'll stop being illustrative in the order the implementation plan lays out.
